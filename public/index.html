How to Play

You are the jockey. Your horse produces the power you ask for — if it has the stamina left to give it. The race is won by pacing: ride too hard too early and your horse fades before the post; wait too long and you finish full of running.

W / SAsk for more / less effort (0–100%). The horse responds gradually: it accelerates, and it settles rather than brakes.
ESettle (hold): the horse relaxes and travels more efficiently, and stops pulling — but it won't quicken until you wake it up, and it gets less from urging and driving. Calm stayers settle quickly; hot sprinters slowly. Only works at a sensible pace.
QCheck (hold): take a firm hold to slow quickly — to avoid running up a horse's heels, drop in behind one, or wait for a gap. Momentum lost costs energy to regain, and a hot horse resents being checked too much.
RLook behind (hold) — you keep full control while looking.
A / DSteer (hold) left / right; release to hold your line anywhere across the track. You fit between two horses only if the gap is wide enough — pick it early. Fast lateral moves at speed cost a little energy. A always steers your horse to its left and D to its right, on every course. On a left-handed course (Flemington) the inside rail is on your left; on a right-handed course (Royal Randwick) it is on your right — the radar shows the rail on the side it really is.
SpaceBefore the gates: hold/release to keep the marker in the green; then press the instant the gates open. Mid-race: urge — a short push that costs energy and sours the horse if overused. Final 450 m: tap to the stride rhythm.
ShiftDrive: commit to a hard, energy-hungry riding style for the finish.

Travelling well. Every horse has a hidden efficiency zone — the lighter band on the effort bar. Inside it the horse cruises; above it stamina drains sharply and fatigue builds. Fatigue never recovers mid-race: it lowers top speed, dulls acceleration and blunts your finish. The zone shrinks as the horse tires. Stayers hold a higher zone than sprinters, which is why a sprinter must be nursed over a long trip.

Sprint reserve is the finishing burst. Hard effort, urging and driving unlock it, but it only improves how well the horse accesses what it has left — an exhausted horse cannot sprint, however hard you ride.

The start has two parts. 1 · Get ready: while the horses load and the countdown runs, a green sweet spot drifts along the start meter. Hold Space to raise your marker and release it to let the marker sink; keeping it in the green builds readiness (a calm horse gives you a wider green). 2 · React: the gates open after an unpredictable pause — press Space again the instant they do. Readiness and reaction time together decide the break: PERFECT (worth about a length or more), GOOD, SLOW AWAY (several lengths) or MISSED THE START (stumbles, near the back). Pressing before you could have seen the gates counts as a guess. Your horse's starting ability and temperament shift the odds, and rivals can miss the break too. It only affects how quickly the horse gets going — never its top speed — and it matters most in sprints. Use W/S beforehand to set your start effort: bolder jumps quicker but costs energy.

The final drive: inside the last 450 m a pin sweeps side to side. Tap Space each time it passes through the green. Consecutive good taps build momentum: Finding rhythm (1–2) → Strong drive (3–4) → Powerful finish (5–6) → Perfect rhythm (7+). The more accurate the taps, the more of its remaining power the horse can use — a perfect drive is worth several lengths. A slightly early or late tap only dents momentum; a tap in the grey breaks the streak, and two in a row unbalance the horse. Momentum can only spend what is left: a horse you ran into the ground earlier gains very little from it.

Position. On the bends the inside rail is genuinely shorter: every lane wider covers more ground, and the HUD counts the metres you have given away (+N m wide). But the rail is where you get pocketed — a boxed-in horse cannot run past the one in front. Racing wide gives you clear room to pass at the cost of ground. Hold A or D to drift across. Calls such as INSIDE BLOCKED, OUTSIDE CLEAR, GAP OPENING, RIVAL CLOSING and LOSING GROUND WIDE tell you what is happening around you, and the radar at the bottom right shows horses on your inside, outside, in front (red when blocking) and closing from behind (flashing).

Balance and incidents. A galloping horse can only take so much sideways load. Steering hard through a bend at speed, weaving, snatching up with Q mid-bend, leaning on a rival (advanced steering) or clipping heels all unsettle it — more so on soft or heavy ground, for a tired horse or a less agile one. A Balance chip appears when it's unsettled, with warnings: DANGEROUS TURN, UNBALANCED, LOSING STRIDE. Straighten up and ease off and it recovers; keep pushing and it will stumble (costing speed and control) and, with Full incidents on, it can fall — out of the race, a DNF. Speed alone is never dangerous. Assisted steering refuses to push into a horse alongside; advanced steering lets you force it — and pay for it. Set it in Settings → Racing incidents (Off · Stumbles only · Full); the same rules apply to every runner.

Race phases. The phase tag under the timer follows the race: the opening scramble for position, the middle where you save energy and hold your place, the final bend where rival riders decide whether to hold the rail, angle out for a run or follow a move, then the home straight and the final 200 m. The rhythm drive starts 450 m out.

Rival jockeys each have their own personality — some go from the front, some wait, some follow your move or refuse to give way — and they change plans as the race unfolds. They obey exactly the same physics and stamina rules as you.

After the race you see a replay of the finish from the recorded race. Close finishes get the slow-motion photo-finish treatment with the exact margin. Study it like a judge: Space pause/play, ←/→ step frame by frame (1/240 s; hold Shift for 0.1 s), drag the timeline, L jumps to the moment at the line, scroll or +/− to zoom in (up to 16×) and drag to pan, C changes angle, M toggles the measurement — a line through each nose with the gap in centimetres, lengths and seconds. Enter or Esc closes it.
